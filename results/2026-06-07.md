**EU-UK ETS Linking Deal — July 13 Summit Now the Key Date for European Carbon Markets**


The EU-UK summit, reportedly scheduled for July 13, is expected to confirm a formal link between the two emissions trading systems — which would be the most significant structural change to European carbon markets since the UK left the EU ETS in 2021.
 
European carbon markets are already pricing in this shift: the EUA–UKA price spread had narrowed to below €13/t, its lowest since February, and UKA prices surged close to 6% in a single day on May 26 alone.
 
EUAs are expected to remain range-bound around €75–80/t in the near term, with policy reform uncertainty limiting directional moves until the Commission publishes its EU ETS proposals in July.

**⚠️ SECOND-ORDER RISK: UK Political Crisis — Starmer Leadership Under Severe Threat**


UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer's leadership is hanging by a thread as a growing number of his own party's lawmakers call on the Labour leader to resign, following a badly performing Labour Party in local elections.
 
Eurasia Group raised the probability of Starmer being ousted this year to 80%, with only a 20% chance of him remaining PM.
 This is highly material for carbon markets: 
the most prominent near-term risk for UKA prices is UK domestic politics — uncertainty around Starmer's tenure has weighed on UKA prices in recent months, with Energy Aspects lowering its Q2 2026 UKA price forecast by £1/t on this political risk.
 
A leadership challenge could delay the summit, though broad cross-party support for closer EU integration means even a change of leader may not derail linkage — but if the summit is pushed to end-2026, UKA prices could average £57/t in H2 2026 vs. £71/t in the base case.

**EU ETS Benchmark Update — Commission Aims to Adopt by End of June**


The European Commission announced proposed updated EU ETS benchmark values for 2026–2030, including measures to implement expanded free allocation of allowances covering indirect emissions from electricity use, anticipated to provide around €4 billion in additional value to industry through 2030.
 
The Commission aims to adopt the ETS benchmarks by the end of June, following a 4-week consultation and scrutiny by EU member states.
 This is bearish for near-term EUA demand, as higher free allocation reduces the need for companies to purchase allowances on the market.

**10 EU Member States Push to Extend Free Carbon Permits Beyond 2034**


In March 2026, ten EU member states called on the European Commission to extend the allocation of free carbon permits to industrial sectors beyond 2034. Signatories — including Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, the Czech Republic, Greece, Hungary, Italy, Poland, Romania, and Slovakia — cited surging energy costs and industrial burdens.
 
Separately, six countries have been asking the EU to protect heavy industry from carbon emission costs in a letter reviewed by the FT — the Czech Republic, Bulgaria, Poland, Romania, Greece, and Slovakia — calling for an increase in the number of carbon allowances within the ETS and arguing that climate policy must adapt to geopolitical realities and extremely high energy prices.

**MSR Reform Proposal Tabled — Supply Stability Mechanism Under Review**


On April 1, 2026, the Commission tabled a legislative proposal to revise the Market Stability Reserve (MSR), the mechanism that adjusts the supply of allowances in the EU ETS to stabilise the carbon market by removing or releasing permits depending on market conditions.
 
Under the current system, all allowances in the reserve above 400 million are invalidated; the proposed amendment would stop this invalidation mechanism, allowing these allowances to be kept as a buffer to support market stability.
 
Energy Aspects' post-reform risk case lowers the average EUA price over 2026–30 by €7/t, with the reform process adding almost 400 Mt of supply over 2026–30 in the risk scenario.

## 🗞️ Big Picture

July 2026 is shaping up to be a watershed month for European carbon markets. The EU-UK ETS linking agreement — potentially the most significant structural change since Brexit — is expected to be announced at the July 13 summit, but is directly threatened by the accelerating political crisis surrounding UK PM Starmer, whose probability of being ousted before year-end has been put at 80% by Eurasia Group, creating meaningful downside risk for UKA prices. Simultaneously, the EU Commission is set to publish a comprehensive ETS revision proposal in July — covering MSR reform, free allocation rules, carbon removals, and linkage criteria — and a bloc of 10 member states is lobbying hard for more generous free allocations and weaker carbon costs, injecting further bearish supply-side risk into EUA prices. The Commission's near-term benchmark update (due by end-June) adding ~€4bn in free allowances is also marginally bearish for near-term EUA demand. On the UKA side, the July 1 maritime scope expansion adds structural demand support, but the political overhang is the dominant near-term price driver — making UK domestic politics arguably the single most important non-market variable to watch right now.
