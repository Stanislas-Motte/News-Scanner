## 2. News

### 1. RGGI — Q2 Auction Clears Well Below Secondary Prices; Affordability Review Triggered (RGGI / Carbon Pulse — Fri Jun 5)


The Q2 RGGI auction cleared well below secondary prices, and RGGI states announced they will begin an affordability process.
 
Ahead of the June 3 sale, the auction was widely expected to result in clears between several dollars below or above current secondary market prices, which had fluctuated at historic highs amid unprecedented volatility
 — with secondary futures having reached as high as $58.50/short ton in early May before falling sharply following a market monitoring notice from the RGGI states. 
Virginia's participation is set to resume July 1, 2026, with the state's allowance allocation and participation in the September 9 and December 2 auctions already noted.
 The combination of a sharp auction undershoot, the Virginia re-entry expanding the cap, and the launch of an affordability process introduces meaningful bearish uncertainty for RGA prices near-term, while also raising longer-term program integrity questions.

### 2. EUAs — Profit-Taking Pulls Prices Lower; H2 Auction Supply Hike Takes Effect (Carbon Pulse / EEX — Mon Jun 2 / May 13)


European carbon allowance prices dropped back below an important psychological level on Monday amid profit-taking after the market had risen to a nearly 16-week high on Friday, as daily auction volumes rose and are expected to increase further next month, while fading hopes for a quick ceasefire agreement in the Strait of Hormuz also added to bearish pressure.
 The supply overhang is material: 
the revised EEX auction calendar for H2 2026, incorporating 50 million allowances for the Social Climate Fund, results in a 12% increase in auction volumes compared with the original schedule.
 
The carbon market reacted negatively to the announcement, with EUA prices moving lower as traders assessed the impact of the higher auction volumes; market participants viewed the revised calendar as a bearish signal, with one analyst noting "the downtrend continues on reports of higher allowance allocations."
 Separately, 
on June 2, EUAs reversed an early decline as the market focused on a key technical level ahead of the fortnightly pause in the auction programme, with sources expressing consensus that prices just below the €80.00 level are appropriate for the moment.

### 3. EUAs — MSR TNAC Published: 190.5 Million Allowances to Enter Reserve from September 2026 (European Commission — Sun Jun 1)


The European Commission adopted the TNAC Communication; the TNAC is the annual surplus indicator determining the functioning of the MSR. In 2025, the TNAC stood at 1,023,494,202 allowances.
 
In line with the MSR Decision, 190,494,202 allowances will be placed in the MSR over 12 months, from September 1, 2026 to August 31, 2027.
 This is approximately 85 million fewer permits than the 275.5 million withdrawn in the September 2025–August 2026 period, meaning the MSR's supply-tightening effect on auctions will be meaningfully smaller in the coming year. 
The reduction in auction volumes will be reflected in updated auction calendars around July 2026.
 The smaller withdrawal is a net bearish signal relative to last year's intake, though it remains a structural support for EUA prices.

### 4. UKAs / EUAs — EU-UK ETS Linkage Summit Reportedly Set for July 13 (Energy Aspects — Thu Jun 5)


The EU-UK summit is reportedly scheduled for July 13 and is expected to confirm a formal link between the two emissions trading systems — which would be the most significant structural change to European carbon markets since the UK left the EU ETS in 2021.
 
European carbon markets are already pricing in this shift: the EUA-UKA spread had narrowed to below €13/t, its lowest since early February, and UKA prices surged close to 6% on May 26 alone versus a 1% rise in EUAs.
 Key risks: 
UK domestic politics remain the most prominent near-term risk, with uncertainty around Keir Starmer's tenure weighing on UKA prices in recent months; however, even a change of Labour leader is not expected to derail linkage, given broad cross-party support.
 
In the risk scenario, post-reform ETS changes could lower average EUA prices over 2026–2030 by €7/t, with the reform process adding almost 400 Mt of supply over that period.

### 5. WCAs / CCAs — Washington Formally Proposes Linkage Regulations (WA Dept. of Ecology — Mon Jun 2)


On June 1, the Washington Department of Ecology proposed regulatory amendments to support future linkage of Washington's Cap-and-Invest Program with the joint California-Quebec market under WCI. Building on a draft linkage agreement released in March 2026, the proposal would align key market rules including compliance timelines, allowance tracking, and accounting procedures.
 
These milestones position Washington to sign a linkage agreement in 2026 and begin operating a shared market with California and Québec in 2027.
 The price impact for WCAs is structurally bearish: 
the proposed linkage could represent a significant shift for Washington's Cap-and-Invest Program, which has historically operated as a standalone market with tighter supply-demand conditions and higher allowance prices; by enabling cross-jurisdictional trading, linkage may increase liquidity, reduce price volatility, and narrow Washington's pricing premium relative to the broader WCI market.
 
Public comments on the proposed regulations are invited until July 17, 2026.

### 6. EUAs — Benchmark Consultation Closes June 8; Adoption Due End of June (European Commission / Carbon Pulse)


The Commission's public consultation on updated ETS benchmarks for 2026–2030 free allocation ran from May 7 to June 8, 2026.
 
Following the four-week consultation and scrutiny by EU Member States in the Climate Change Committee, the Commission intends to adopt the benchmarks by the end of June 2026.
 
The proposal covers the expanded free allocation of allowances to indirect emissions from electricity use, anticipated by the Commission to provide around €4 billion in additional value to industry through 2030.
 The consultation closing today is a process milestone; formal adoption and the July ETS reform package remain the more significant near-term price catalysts.

### 7. RGGI — Virginia Re-Entry Set for July 1; Adds Supply and Complexity (RGGI States — Apr 29)


On April 29, the RGGI states confirmed Virginia's re-entry, with Governor Spanberger approving Virginia's regulation reinstating its CO₂ budget trading program effective July 1, 2026, including Virginia's participation in the September 9 and December 2 auctions and an addition to the Cost Containment Reserve allowances for the remainder of 2026.
 Combined with the auction undershoot and the affordability review, Virginia's re-entry adds allowance supply and new compliance demand simultaneously — the net market impact will depend on the scale of the state's allocation relative to its compliance obligations.
